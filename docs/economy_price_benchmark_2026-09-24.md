# The Picture Shop: real-world price benchmark

**Prepared:** September 24, 2026

**Market basis:** U.S. public asking/list prices in U.S. dollars; online source pages checked September 24, 2026.
**Purpose:** establish a sourced baseline for balancing game prices. This is a comparison report, not a price change proposal.

## Findings at a glance

- The strongest direct matches are black letterpress ink and Windmill tympan. Both are already close to current specialist-supplier prices.
- Capital equipment is the largest apparent mismatch. The three game machine offers and forklift are much cheaper than public used listings or new comparable equipment. The in-game machine amounts look more like progression-friendly game prices than real equipment prices.
- Paper and packaging results are mixed. House paper looks close after a cautious sheet-area adjustment; cover stock looks materially cheap against current large-sheet cover listings. Carton and stretch-film comparisons are weak because the game does not specify size, strength, length, or gauge.
- Warehouse construction prices cannot be judged cleanly without real dimensions and scope. The forklift and ten-position rack have usable benchmarks; floor and breakroom amounts need square footage and construction details.
- A number of supplies are named as bundles without a bill of materials. They are not responsibly priceable until their contents and units are defined.

Market prices below are listings, not verified completed transactions. They can omit freight, rigging, taxes, installation, or local labor. Used equipment condition and included accessories vary widely. I use a comparison percentage only when the units and product are close enough to make it informative.

## Machines: new and used

The game computes each machine's asking price from a base value and condition: `base × (0.25 + 0.75 × condition/100)`. The online catalog uses near-new condition; the dealer catalog uses a lower condition and a further 0.84 channel factor. Those two in-game prices are not the same machine-condition offer, so the comparison below keeps them separate.

The job system also adjusts offers for urgency, customer relationship, reputation, and repeat-business discounts. Its generated quote is not a fixed catalog price, so a full job-price benchmark needs a consistent example job, stock responsibility, finish, delivery scope, and turnaround.

## Price-like values that are not active catalog prices

- `src/shop.lua` contains an old sample transaction of $25 for paper and $12 for a print, but it is only exercised by smoke-test code and is not wired into the current player-facing economy. It is excluded from the balancing baseline.
- The game has no employee wage or salary expense in the inventory examined; labor only appears as an input to the press quote estimate.
- No explicit sale prices were found for office furniture or décor. Values used only for game progression, condition, reputation, or inventory are not standalone prices.

## What to standardize before changing prices

The best next pass is to lock down the missing units and scopes: paper dimensions and weight; cover-stock dimensions, weight, and finish; wrapper automation and throughput; forklift capacity and power type; carton dimensions and board strength; stretch-film width, length, and gauge; warehouse square footage; and the contents of each bundled kit. Then refresh supplier quotes for the weakly matched categories and decide which game prices should represent purchase cost, installed cost, or a simplified progression cost. That will let the next balance pass adjust the complete economy without mixing unlike units.
