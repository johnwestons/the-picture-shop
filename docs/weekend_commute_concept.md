## Proposed experience

1. Finish Friday's existing shop shutdown checklist. The main entrance offers **Exit warehouse** when departure is permitted.
2. Enter a small exterior parking-lot scene on foot, using the selected character's normal appearance. Walk to the player's parked trail bike. The warehouse door permits going back inside before departure.
3. Choose **Put riding gear on** beside the bike. Show the selected character stepping into/pulling on the one-piece suit, inserting arms, visibly pulling the front zipper closed, then donning and securing the full-face helmet. Boots and gloves must also be equipped before completion. Do not hide dressing behind a fade. After the sequence, **Mount & ride home** becomes available as a separate action: mount, start the engine, settle into idle, then transition to the town road. Fully equipped players can mount without repeating dressing.
4. Ride freely through a fixed, small street network to the selected apartment's address. This is travel, with acceleration, braking and steering, rather than a scored minigame. First trial excludes traffic, errands, fuel economy, crashes and buildings.
5. Stop inside the apartment parking area. Offer **Park & dismount** only when stopped. Engine stops; the character dismounts still wearing the complete suit, boots, gloves and helmet. They can walk around and use a separate **Enter apartment** action while geared. The home/weekend arrival event occurs exactly once on entering the apartment.
6. When stationary on foot, choose **Take riding gear off** separately, including inside the apartment. Show helmet removal, unzipping and removing the one-piece suit, then restore the selected character's normal appearance and action set. Dismounting, arrival and door transitions must never remove gear automatically. Gear stays in personal inventory for the concept, so removal inside cannot strand the player away from equipment stored at the bike; physical gear storage can be designed later.

## Shared rider, including future characters

The fully geared appearance is a shared stylized animal avatar. **The user rejected the original short/chibi rider proportions:** use the suit-up sprites as the authoritative torso, limb and helmet-to-body reference. Preserve their longer torso and legs, animal-shaped feet and moderate helmet size; enlarging the old squat drawing does not satisfy this correction. The same body must carry through standing, walking and mounted views. Broad, slightly elongated paw-shaped feet are completely enclosed in protective riding boots; no bare paws or protruding claws. Use a visibly continuous one-piece leather garment with a front zipper, full-face helmet with closed visor, gloves and boots. The helmet conceals ears and face; the closed suit conceals fur and tail. This shared animal-like silhouette can serve future playable species without becoming a realistic human figure. No riding state may show work clothes or incomplete gear.

Keep character identity/customization in player state. Equipment appearance is independent of being mounted, being outdoors and weekend phase: a fully geared character uses the same shared avatar while standing, walking, entering buildings or riding. Restore the original avatar only after the explicit removal animation. Multiplayer players can share art while remaining distinct player entities.

Visible dressing changes the earlier asset-saving assumption: exposed heads/hands and the pre-suit body belong to the selected character. Start with rabbit-specific dressing/removal transitions and preserve its customization; future species need matching transition overlays or a small species-specific dressing set. Fully covered idle, walk, mount, ride and dismount remain shared. Do not show every future species turning into a rabbit while putting the helmet on. Coordinate the selected character head disappearing under the helmet with the actual donning frames.

## Gear states, actions and small animation set

Proposed states: normal → putting on suit → zipping up → putting on helmet → geared on foot → mounted. Dismount returns to geared on foot. Explicit removal proceeds through helmet off → unzipping/removing suit → normal. Dressing intermediates are short controlled animations rather than freely playable outfits in the initial version.

| State | Available actions | Restrictions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal on foot | Existing character actions; put gear on by bike | Cannot mount/ride until complete gear sequence finishes |
| Dressing/removing | Finish current animation | Block conflicting movement, mounting and household actions; interruption returns to a coherent equipped or unequipped state |
| Fully geared on foot | Idle, walk, turn, simple door interaction, enter/exit buildings, mount by bike, take gear off while stationary | Eating/drinking blocked; initially block sleep, bathing, cooking, work and other activities without a suited animation |
| Mounted | Ride, brake, stop, park/dismount | No gear removal, eating, household actions or ordinary pedestrian door entry |

Use clear prompts such as **Take off your riding gear to eat**. Do not silently auto-unequip or make a geared player use an unsuited action sprite. The minimal on-foot set is directional idle/walk with grounded foot contact, a brief turn/stop if needed, and a minimal door reach or existing no-pose door transition. No eating or domestic-activity animations in the helmet. Walking is a normal critter stride, not a hopping gait or human march; synchronize planted boots and travel distance.

Review individual activity restrictions later if desired; the initial allowlist intentionally limits the sprite workload. Reuse normal interaction/navigation controls for geared walking so the player can enter the apartment and choose when to change.

## Terrain-first town layout

The town is an irregular settlement within a landscape, not a square perimeter road. Working scale remains roughly **a quarter mile across**, around a 402 × 402 m planning envelope. This is a size budget, not the street layout, coastline or playable boundary. Use surrounding generated scenery beyond the commute area to avoid exposing a square edge. If the original size meant an area of 0.25 square mile, that would be roughly 805 m across; keep scale configurable until the first terrain study establishes useful distances.

Set the environment in the same southeastern North American biome as Critter Party. Use rolling uplands, a modest river valley, wooded slopes, grass clearings and damp river margins. Do not flatten the whole map or shrink every island mountain/ridge into an over-steep miniature. Start with a town terrain preset that preserves the existing generator's terrain character at the smaller scale. An initial 10–25 m total relief is a gameplay tuning hypothesis, not a measured regional elevation model or a settled requirement.

Proposed composition, to be refined against actual generated terrain:

- A river meanders diagonally along the eastern part of the town area, with a wooded low bank and a higher terrace suitable for roads and lots.
- The warehouse sits on a locally graded service pad at the southwest edge of the settlement, above the wet low ground. A short access spur meets the main road.
- Main Street bends along the valley terrace, with a short straighter stretch reserved for future shops and groceries. It does not trace the map boundary.
- One bridge crosses at a narrow, stable-looking part of the valley. Approaches rise gradually to its deck instead of plunging into the river channel.
- A residential road winds uphill around the western ridge. Another follows the eastern bank beyond the bridge. Staggered T-junctions, unequal block lengths and a few short spurs make the network asymmetric. Add a connected inland loop for route choice; do not create an unnecessary second bridge just to complete a square.
- Preserve wooded gaps, small clearings and river-edge foliage between future plots. Roads should have purposeful bends following terrain, not arbitrary noise on every segment.

| Existing destination ID | Proposed setting |
| --- | --- |
| `brick-walkup` | Lower-town terrace near the future commercial stretch; relatively direct warehouse access |
| `garden` | Quieter east-bank clearing beyond the bridge, with space for a garden |
| `courtyard` | Sheltered mid-slope neighborhood reached by a curved residential spur |
| `corner` | Higher road junction at the western ridge shoulder, with a different approach and view |

These are proposed relationships, not finalized coordinates. Remove the previous square-grid coordinates and commute-distance claims from implementation inputs. Generate/select terrain first, then establish safe lots, the crossing, street curves and measured route lengths. Target a short, pleasant commute, provisionally about one to two minutes depending on home, without artificially lengthening roads just to consume time.

The finished town remains identical across new saves. During development, choose and refine one seed plus explicit terrain/river controls, then freeze the approved terrain settings, road graph and destination anchors. Procedural generation builds that authored world consistently; it does not reroll the town per save. Version generator code, terrain data and road edits together because a seed alone cannot preserve the map after generator changes.

Keep internal terrain bounds aligned with the existing coarse sampling/patch grids and pad them beyond the playable area; 402 m need not be an exact generator array dimension. The current plan uses an 8-unit coarse grid and the streamed world uses 16-unit patches, so exact internal sizing needs an explicit adapter decision rather than blindly replacing the island size constant.

## Build the landscape, then fit the town to it

1. Generate the base hills, valley, water corridor and habitat fields using the adapted Critter Party environment systems. A town preset supplies inland extents and chosen landscape controls; avoid an accidental island/ocean boundary around the settlement.
2. Choose dry buildable terraces and a bridge crossing from that terrain. Freeze the warehouse and apartment anchors only after checking slopes, river banks and road access.
3. Lay out connected road curves around those features. Smooth the longitudinal road profile and joins; apply localized cut/fill and shoulder blending. Keep elevation variation between neighborhoods. A tentative ordinary-road grade target of 6–8 percent, with occasional short steeper sections only if driving feels good, is a game tuning proposal rather than a civil-engineering standard.
4. Build asphalt, shoulders, intersections, markings and parking pads from the same road definition used by collision and vehicle support. Slight visual offsets can prevent overlapping surfaces, but must not become different physical road heights.
5. Represent the bridge deck as its own supported surface above the water/riverbed. One ground-height value at x/z cannot represent both the deck and channel. The initial trial needs a traversable deck, smooth ramps and edge collision; decorative bridge detail can wait.
6. Place vegetation from the existing habitat distribution, with town exclusion masks for pavement, shoulders, lot footprints, bridge approaches and junction sightlines. Use lower density in settled areas and fuller woodland along slopes and the river.
7. Stream/render ground, water, road surfaces and sprites through a common camera/depth convention. Validate the resulting landscape from multiple camera angles before adding finished buildings.

For the first trial, reuse existing water visuals with a coherent fixed-level river corridor if necessary; do not imply the current generator already simulates downstream flow, floods or sloping water surfaces. A descending river profile or multiple water levels would be separate work. Preserve the channel under bridge grading and stop pavement/vegetation masks from filling it in.

## Proposed rendering and control approach

Carry over Critter Party's camera capabilities: orbit/yaw, tilt, zoom, pan/recenter, smooth following and terrain-aware projection. Start from an elevated oblique default view, but do not discard orbit controls or assume a permanently locked heading. Reuse its input behavior where it fits The Picture Shop, including touch gestures, then tune motorcycle follow height, speed-dependent look-ahead and zoom response. Unrelated hawk/flight logic should stay out of the town camera adapter.

Ground/topography, water and roads are generated geometry/materials; bike, rider, trees and foliage remain sprites. Select rider/bike direction relative to camera yaw rather than world heading alone. Start by validating existing camera ranges with the sprite pilot; restrict extreme pitch only where the billboard art fails, or add camera-angle art if needed. Ordinary yaw changes need appropriate directional views, whereas steep pitch variation can require additional art beyond simply increasing heading count. Check canopy occlusion, slope ordering and the readable silhouette of a small rider behind trees.

Critter Party already selects animal directions relative to camera yaw. Its plant sprites remain screen-facing billboards with authored ground pivots, rather than changing to a new tree view at every camera angle. Preserve that established look for imported trees. This does not automatically solve orbitable building art; future buildings will need their own multi-view or geometry-plus-texture decision, as originally discussed.

Maintain simulation positions in town metres and project them once into screen space. Generate road geometry and collision from the same street definition. Use one authoritative support query that distinguishes terrain, graded road and bridge deck; use it for tire contact, pedestrian feet, placement, camera focus and interaction picking. Terrain-only unprojection must not select riverbed below a bridge. Keep physical support stable across rendering LOD changes and reconcile visual ground meshes with it so tires do not float as chunks simplify. Merge intersections cleanly and keep markings in world coordinates to prevent sliding textures, seams or doubled paint.

Sample front and rear tire contact on hills. Start with modest drivable grades and a restrained visual pitch/lean treatment compatible with directional sprites, then assess whether uphill/downhill poses are needed. A flat-road side sprite can visibly float on a slope; camera freedom and terrain both need to be proven before committing to the full motorcycle atlas. Ground shadows follow the support surface; vegetation roots remain on actual terrain, not on a bridge deck.

Start with 8 rider/bike headings for a trial; move to 16 if turning looks too stepped. Choose headings relative to camera orientation and use hysteresis near boundaries. Do not rotate a side-view sprite continuously as a substitute for actual directional art. Use acceleration, speed-sensitive steering, braking and a gentle off-road slowdown. Test tire contact and camera motion at the highest planned speed and lowest frame rate.

Keep render distance configurable, but derive visible ground/chunk coverage from viewport, zoom, orbit, tilt, terrain elevation and look-ahead. Stream enough surrounding terrain to hide the settlement boundary; do not expose blank wedges at screen corners or behind a crest. Reuse chunk caching, bounded generation budgets and prop streaming, tuning prefetch for vehicle speeds rather than copying animal movement settings unchanged. Trees, water and moving-camera chunk transitions belong in the first performance trial. No performance result is established by this plan or the superseded schematic.
