**Weekend apartments and personal life — implementation proposal**

Prepared October 31, 2026, from the current working source; revised November 1 with the user's approved direction. This is a design and implementation plan; initial art remains concept-only until reviewed. Four apartment variants are the target. Empty apartments are the default, modest furnished starts are optional, and apartment condition and rent vary by variant. The times, dollar amounts, and payout defaults below are recommendations for the first version.

**1. The playable loop**

Keep one continuous calendar and one host-owned save. Add a household scene and a business operating state: working, closing, weekend at home, and returning to work. Home is a world scene, not a computer menu; characters keep their existing walking, camera, interaction, and input systems.

| Point in the week | Proposed behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Friday evening | Announce closing ahead of time. Proposed initial closing time: 20:00. Show a short shutdown checklist and the personal-pay preview. |
| Closing | Stop admitting new work, complete or safely pause active machine steps, park transport, release workstations, and retain unfinished jobs. Finish actual paid work before stopping employee wage accrual. |
| Ready to leave | Hold the calendar after safe shutdown so walking to the exit does not consume the weekend. Player movement and departure controls remain active. At the exterior front entrance, show **Go home for the weekend**. |
| Departure | Host selects Go home, guests receive a ready prompt, the party transitions directly to the apartment, and the calendar resumes at the saved closing time. No travel map in this version. |
| Saturday and Sunday | Shop stays closed. Players cook, eat, drink, bathe, sleep, clean, shop, unpack deliveries, and arrange furniture. |
| Sunday evening / Monday morning | Remind everyone about work. Offer sleep until Monday morning; proposed wake time 07:00 and shop opening 09:00. Show **Return to shop** at the apartment door on Monday morning. |
| Return | Host brings the group back, places players at clear entrance positions, and resumes work no earlier than opening time. Returning late opens the shop late; it never rewinds the clock. |

Closing must not require finishing an entire production order. Use each machine's safe interruption point, preserving work and material ownership. If a machine cannot stop immediately, show the exact remaining shutdown step; never leave an indefinite generic wait. Check for ongoing service work, carried pallets, warehouse-room players, open machine GUIs, and departing pickup trucks. A completed pickup and its payment must commit before the weekly payout is calculated.

Departing guests do not have to pathfind to the door individually. Notify them and wait for readiness; the host may use a clearly labeled **Bring everyone home** action after outstanding interactions are canceled safely. This also prevents an idle player from trapping the session. The same rule applies on Monday. Guests cannot independently reopen the shop on Saturday.

**2. Time, business closure, and needs**

Reuse the existing save-specific 5–60 real minutes per game day, with its 20-minute default. At normal speed two weekend days take 40 real minutes on the default setting before sleeping or acceleration. Keep the clock, weekday, next work time, and four need bars visible at home. An optional clock prop is decoration and an alarm control; time and basic alarm access remain available through the HUD/laptop even when no clock prop is owned.

The calendar continues while at home. Shop production, staff work, physical deliveries, visitors, and on-site construction/service work do not. Shop bills, loan due dates, home rent, and already-earned payroll obligations still advance on calendar time and settle through their existing ledgers. Email may arrive and future business orders may be placed remotely, but fulfillment waits for business opening. No offline elapsed-time simulation is introduced.

Shift availability and payroll forecasts must intersect employee contracts with actual shop opening periods. New scheduling UI must explain the closed weekend. Existing weekend or Friday-night shifts must not generate phantom paid hours or require employees to attend a locked shop; retain earned balances, show the new effective schedule, and use the next valid shift. Monday-to-Thursday overnight work can remain supported.

Separate business-time deadlines from calendar-time obligations. Customer jobs described in business days must skip Saturdays and Sundays; a calendar-month rent bill must not. Store explicit due timestamps and deadline policy rather than repeatedly parsing display text. Friday arrivals postponed to Monday should retain their queue and contents without duplicate dispatch or unfair lateness. Audit construction, repair appointments, quote expiry, and supply delivery policies individually.

| Need | Restored by | First-version consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hunger | Groceries, cooked meals, takeout | Clear reminders, then a capped stamina/comfort penalty |
| Thirst | Sink water in a cup or glass; purchased drinks | Clear reminders and a capped penalty |
| Hygiene | Shower/bath, handwashing; supported by clean clothes | Comfort penalty and visible hygiene feedback |
| Energy | Bed, couch nap, temporary sleeping mat | Tired animation and capped movement/action penalty |

Use game-time rates for needs and activity durations. Keep actions usable at the shortest day setting: no long real-time minigame should silently consume an entire day. Start with forgiving values and no death or eviction loop. Tap water is always available, basic floor rest works without a furniture item, and rent grace gives a cash-poor player time to recover. Floor rest is less effective than a bed or couch. Empty starts do not silently receive groceries, beds, mats, cups, or other furnishings; show affordable essentials in the laptop onboarding and tune the starter allowance to cover them.

Needs belong to individual characters. For this weekend-focused release, weekday routine automatically covers basic maintenance, so players are not required to leave production to manage hunger without weekday food facilities. Freeze absent guests' needs. Expose a Sunday readiness summary; tune any Monday performance bonus separately after the core economy works.

Sleep can pass time in solo play. In multiplayer, resting one person does not skip another person's activity. Accelerated shared sleep requires all connected players to opt in; an awake or newly joined player interrupts it. Offline players do not vote. Skip processing advances rent, deliveries, needs, and alarms in chronological steps, without running closed-shop production or charging an event twice. Stop at the Monday wake reminder. Ordinary GUIs follow the existing running-clock behavior; a pause command, if added, is explicit and shared.

**3. Business money and household money**

Keep the existing business balance as business money. Add a separate household balance used for apartment rent, food, takeout, furniture, and home upgrades. Start with one household account belonging to the hosted save: guests share the home and its purchases. Individual transferable guest bank accounts can be a later feature.

Add **Computer → Finances → Personal pay** with a host-editable 0–100% slider, business retained percentage, earned amount, protected reserves, next payout, and transaction history. Recommend a 25% starting setting, clearly presented as adjustable. The laptop can show the same page; guest access follows permissions.

Recommend applying the percentage to cash-backed earnings after costs. Applying it to every dollar of sales would let material purchases and unpaid wages appear spendable. The wording should say exactly which basis is used.

For the initial design:

- Record actual collected job revenue, matched consumed materials, earned wages, accrued operating expenses, and refunds/claims in a period ledger. Quotes, uncollected invoices, loans, and starting capital are not earnings. Inventory purchases and later material consumption cannot both be deducted as the same expense.
- Determine distributable earnings for that week, carrying negative adjustments forward. Do not reuse the current Shop Budget forecast as an actual profit ledger.
- Compute requested personal pay as the chosen percentage of positive distributable earnings. Cap it at cash available after unpaid obligations and the configured operating reserve. Include upcoming payroll, bills, and loan payments in the reserve preview; debt principal affects cash availability, not operating profit a second time.
- At Friday shutdown, transfer the final amount from business to household exactly once. Show reductions caused by the reserve. Unpaid requested draws are not silently turned into company debt.
- Save the reporting period, applied rate, ledger checkpoint, and transfer together. Reloading, reconnecting, or changing the slider cannot pay the same earnings twice. New percentage changes apply to the next unsettled period.

Example: $1,000 of distributable earnings at 25% gives $250 to the household and leaves $750 in the business, provided the cash reserve allows it. The screen must show the dollar amount, not only percentages.

Give a new household a separately identified starter living allowance and a first-rent grace period. Basic groceries are included only in the optional furnished starter package; empty-start players buy their own. This is especially important because the current calendar starts on a Thursday and the first Friday may contain little income. Size the allowance against at least the first weekend's essential purchases. Keep the allowance consistent across apartment choices so higher rent is a visible budget tradeoff. Recommend monthly apartment rent, with the due date and weekly saving target visible; arrears receive reminders and a grace period in the first release.

**4. Four starter apartments**

All four have a kitchen, living area, one separate bedroom, bathroom, closet, entrance, and laundry fixture, with enough circulation for four players. They vary in finish, wear, layout, and rent, while remaining modest starter apartments. The least expensive is visibly shabby but habitable; the highest tier is clean and recently refreshed, not luxurious. Show condition and rent before selection. The following rents are provisional game-balance values, not real-world market estimates.

| Variant | Condition and proposed monthly rent | Look and layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B — Brick walk-up | Shabby / $450 | Old exposed brick and scuffed plaster, patched vinyl, worn fittings; galley kitchen right, living room left, short hall to bedroom |
| D — Garden apartment | Worn but serviceable / $600 | Faded green/cream paint, older tile and wood, modest windows; kitchen alcove, bedroom rear left, bathroom and closet rear right |
| A — Courtyard | Fair and cared for / $800 | Warm plaster and wood, tidy older fittings with light wear; kitchen left, living room front, bedroom and bath at the back |
| C — Corner apartment | Good / $1,000 | Fresh simple paint, inexpensive newer laminate, brighter windows; open kitchen/living area, bedroom opposite entrance, compact central bathroom |

Add a preview/select step to **New game → Shop Setup Options**, including **Random**, a condition label, and monthly rent. Random selects an apartment and its associated rent/condition together, displays the result before creation, and resolves only once in the saved game. Add an independent **Starting furnishings: Empty (default) / Modestly furnished** choice, with an exact contents preview. Randomizing an apartment does not randomize or override that furnishing choice. The home belongs to the save from creation even if the game begins at work and its first visit is Friday.

Generate each apartment as registered transparent layers, following the user's November 1 art revision. The base sprite contains floors, kitchen sink with a short fixed counter, bathroom sink, toilet, tub/shower, and washer/dryer. All walls, wall caps, doors/windows and attached architectural details belong in an independent wall sprite, never painted into the floor base. Every pixel outside the apartment must be transparent; surrounding ground and scenery come from separate future background assets. Keep the approved elevated isometric camera. Reserve a dry reachable end of the kitchen counter for the starter laptop, away from the sink. The laptop itself remains a separate movable object and is absent from the floor sprite. Fixed fixtures still need interactive hotspots, approach positions, occupancy, and saved functional state; painted pixels alone cannot implement them.

Keep a stove/cooktop and additional modular counters as catalog objects, with a dedicated cooking/utility position. The short sink counter is a basic fixed fixture in every empty apartment; additional storage and worktop units are purchases. Plumbing and laundry stay fixed. Utility-dependent upgrades snap only to approved service points, while desks, beds, chairs, and other ordinary furniture use the free placement grid. Prepare aligned fixture replacement overlays and clean repair patches so a future upgraded toilet does not show the old toilet underneath.

Each layout is one playable apartment scene with internal rooms. Preserve full architectural walls in a separate texture and fade only the portion that obscures the locally controlled player, using a soft circular/elliptical reveal radius. Restore opacity as the player leaves. This is a visual effect: wall collision, doors, and room boundaries stay solid. Each multiplayer client computes visibility from its own controlled character and camera; wall transparency does not become shared game state. Depth-sort wall sections and fixed fixtures so rear walls do not cover sinks or showers. Do not create five additional scene-loading transitions inside each small apartment.

**5. Furniture, food, and activity scope**

The full first-release catalog covers all requested furniture:

| Group | Items |
| --- | --- |
| Sleep/storage | Bed, bedside table, wardrobe, cabinet |
| Living | Couch, old TV, coffee table |
| Kitchen/dining | Refrigerator, mini fridge, stove with cooktop, modular countertop, small kitchen table, separate chair |
| Work/lighting | Desk, small desk, floor lamp, desk lamp |
| Maintenance | Trash can |

That is 18 base furniture types. Add the starter laptop, a purchasable clock, and a purchasable simple guest sleeping mat. Recommend eight orientations for movable furniture to match the existing machines: up to 144 base furniture views before laptop, mats, state overlays, and interaction poses. Symmetric views may share art only when they actually look identical. A fridge handle, television screen, or desk drawers cannot be flipped into a false rear view.

**Empty is the default:** provide only the apartment's fixed fixtures and a functional laptop on the dry end of the built-in kitchen counter. No free desk, bed, couch, fridge, chair, lamp, clock, or decorative objects. Players buy and build up the household themselves. The HUD still supplies time, and floor rest plus sink access keep the first visit playable without placing furniture automatically.

**Modestly furnished is optional:** provide a cheap bed, worn couch, mini fridge, small desk with the same laptop, one chair, inexpensive lamp, simple clock, cup, and basic groceries. Use separate movable objects in a layout-specific arrangement, so every piece can later move or be replaced. Avoid expensive matching sets. The apartment's listed rent follows its variant and is not silently changed by the furnishing preset. Preview all starting contents and the starting balance before creating the save. Extra beds and guest mats remain purchasable; floor rest supports guests who do not yet have a sleeping place.

Furniture records need a stable instance ID, catalog type, room/scene, position, direction, condition, storage contents where applicable, and interaction anchors. Desks and tables expose surface slots for laptops, lamps, cups, and plates. A supported object moves with its parent; selling or packing the parent first moves its contents safely to storage. A chair remains independently placeable, with a validated seat-to-table position for eating.

Begin food sprites with bread/sandwich ingredients, cereal, produce, a simple cooked meal, packaged meal, pizza box/slices, Chinese takeout carton, water glass, mug, plate, and drink container. Use real inventory portions: buying, storing, preparing, serving, and consuming must transfer the same items. Guests cannot eat the same last serving simultaneously. Some items need closed/open or full/empty states, not eight floor orientations.

Activities for the first complete release:

- Cook a simple meal: choose ingredients, perform a short preparation/timing task, obtain a portion and quality result. Poor timing reduces meal quality; omit house fires for this version.
- Eat/drink: use a serving from inventory at a chair, table, counter, or couch; fetch drinking water from a sink.
- Shower/handwash/toilet: a brief contextual action or progress interaction with bathroom occupancy. A separate bladder meter is outside the first four-need design.
- Clean: collect trash, wash dishes, and wipe dirty areas using short interactions.
- Laundry: load clothes, wait for the game-time cycle, unload; fixtures remain baked into the scene with overlays for state.
- Rest: sit, watch TV, nap, or sleep. Furniture quality changes comfort and recovery rate.
- Decorate: unpack, move, rotate, place, store, or sell eligible household objects.

**6. Shopping and delivery**

Add **CritterNet → Home & Living**, **Groceries**, **Takeout**, and **My Home** to the reusable computer presentation. The laptop opens that same computer system with a home context, household balance, delivery address, rent/calendar page, and an in-game guide. Ordinary business browsing can remain available, while purchases clearly identify the account and destination.

Furniture checkout shows dimensions, permitted orientations, placement requirements, price, shipping, and arrival time. Orders become persistent home deliveries with a unique order ID. Deliver boxes to a designated entrance area; if that area is full, retain the order in a delivery queue accessible from the laptop. Never block the door, discard an item, or instantiate the same order twice. Allow deliveries to wait safely while everyone is at the shop.

Takeout is ordered from the laptop or a home phone menu. Pizza and Chinese food have a visible arrival estimate and multiple servings. At arrival, a door notification lets a player receive it; a safe drop-off record prevents missed deliveries during sleep or a disconnect. Home orders use household funds and never appear on warehouse procurement pallets.

**8. Multiplayer and persistence**

The current game allows a host plus three guests. All travel to the host's apartment. Each player has separate needs, action progress, seat/bed occupancy, and pose; furniture, pantry, deliveries, and the household balance are shared.

| Action | Guest policy for the initial release |
| --- | --- |
| Walk, eat, drink, wash, rest, cook, clean | Allowed, with validated range and object availability |
| View laptop, home guide, catalog, delivery status | Allowed; laptop sessions are independent GUIs |
| Buy food or takeout | Allowed within a host-set household spending limit |
| Purchase furniture, sell objects, rearrange home | Host can grant separate permissions; disabled until granted |
| Change personal-pay percentage or rent settings | Host only |
| End weekend or change shared time speed | Host-led; readiness required before sleep skips other players' time |

The host validates price, balance, object ownership, scene, distance, inventory, and current revision. Clients submit bounded requests. Purchases, eating, payouts, rent, and deliveries require replay-safe IDs and an atomic saved result. A bed or appliance has a short-lived use reservation. Release reservations on cancel, disconnect, travel, or reload; do not hold the whole apartment or computer UI for one player.

Do not use current network player numbers as permanent resident identities: IDs 2–4 are reused. Add a host-issued resident/reconnect identity with authenticated binding to the admitted connection; a name or client-chosen slot is not sufficient. Keep returning residents' needs in the host's save. A genuinely new guest gets a defined starter condition without cash or groceries being minted on every join. Joining at home spawns at a clear home entrance position; joining during departure waits for the completed transition. A host disconnect ends the shared session under the existing ownership model.

Persist apartment ID, scene and weekly phase, home inventory, placed instances, item contents, household balance, pay setting/ledger checkpoint, rent, home deliveries, resident needs, and alarms. Persist the host's valid home location; current offline loading otherwise returns the player to the warehouse. Recover temporary actions to safe interrupted states rather than restoring stale locks. Increase save schema and multiplayer protocol together when the wire/state contract changes; their current versions are 23 and 47.

Legacy compatibility should initialize a default household and a one-time allowance marker without subtracting business funds unexpectedly or dropping existing work. Development follows the user's preference: at the beginning of implementation/testing that may touch saves, reset only relevant Picture Shop test saves, with no automatic backup. This read-only investigation and plan did not touch saves.

**10. Required acceptance checks**

- Friday closure at 1× and accelerated speeds, including large time steps; no missed or repeated phase boundary. Safe cutter, wrapper, press, maintenance, pallet, and truck states resume correctly Monday.
- Saturday/Sunday cannot start shop work. Closed hours do not create staff wages, shop construction progress, or unfair business-day penalties. Calendar bills and earned payroll still post once.
- Payout at 0%, 25%, and 100%; zero profit, insufficient reserve, refunded work, unpaid wages, rounding, repeat requests, reload, and week/month rollover. No loan or starter cash counted as revenue.
- Needs and actions at minimum/default/maximum day lengths; sleep crossing delivery, rent, midnight, and Monday; no offline or disconnected-guest decay.
- Empty default contains only fixed fixtures and one laptop; optional furnished start contains exactly the previewed modest package. Random apartment choice preserves its rent/condition and the independently chosen furnishing preset across reload.
- All requested furniture directions; narrow passages, rotated footprints, invalid placement, tabletop contents, occupied objects, and safe packing/selling. Mandatory exits and fixture approaches stay reachable.
- Four players competing for the final food portion, bathroom, bed, laptop checkout, and placement; permissions, double requests, stale updates, disconnect/reconnect, replacement guest IDs, late join, and host departure.
- Save/reload while home, closing, sleeping, awaiting delivery, unpacking, or after a purchase; return to the correct phase with no duplicated items, vanished furniture, changed apartment roll, or trapped character.
- Four apartment backgrounds reviewed with player/furniture overlays, exact masks, wall occlusion, night lighting, and readable HUD on desktop and physical Android devices. Forced-mobile rendering alone is not device acceptance.
- Existing warehouse movement/placement, employee scheduling, payroll, production, procurement, machine credit, save validation, and network packet limits remain covered by focused and standard regression checks.

Future homes, mortgages, separate guest households/wallets, weekday commuting, pets, house fires, extensive plumbing replacement, and a large recipe system remain later expansions. The first version should establish a dependable weekend rhythm and give shop earnings a useful personal purpose.
