WAREHOUSE DOCK VISUALS - IMPLEMENTATION AND FEASIBILITY
November 10, 2026

Implemented
The main warehouse dock and purchased room docks now use a procedural sectional
steel shutter and a restricted view of the real warehouse loading apron.

- src/dock_shutter.lua keeps the existing warehouse sprite's worn steel material
  and adds independently moving ribs, seams, hinges, fasteners, scoring, a bottom
  seal and pull handle. Fixed tracks, mounting bolts and a header stay on the
  registered wall plane. Door progress and simulation authority are unchanged.
- src/dock_exterior.lua owns a 256 x 256 reusable buffer and a 16-metre-deep,
  18-metre-wide local patch. It reads actual terrain height, hardscape, road paint,
  nearby buildings, street fixtures and seasonal plants. GPU bounds clipping
  prevents long road markings or nearby meshes from extending the ground patch.
- The view reads physical supplier trucks, outdoor players, their parked bikes
  and available player trucks, plus physical AI motorcycles. Remote players use
  the existing realtime walking/bike/truck presentation. It does not start another
  traffic simulation. Detailed dock cargo sprites remain in use; their matching
  terrain truck is suppressed so a shipment does not appear twice.
- Purchased room doors currently share the main physical loading apron. They do
  not have separate outdoor building anchors. Their shutter geometry follows each
  room's painted aperture, but they are not views of different physical lots.

Performance behavior
- Closed, offscreen or departed scenes do no exterior preparation, actor queries
  or exterior renders. Pending owned image requests are canceled. Previously
  prepared buffers remain cached for reopening. No exterior canvas is allocated
  before first opening; shader programs compile in the existing startup load.
- Reuse the commute/traffic world's CPU metadata if available. A genuinely cold
  observer builds the saved world's metadata cooperatively across frames, with
  terrain and street-infrastructure yield points. No full terrain renderer,
  grass-field renderer, town-wide GPU terrain or town-wide building meshes start.
- Prepare one local terrain strip (of twelve) or one scenery object per step.
  Retain the prior exterior picture until the local ground patch is ready, then
  crossfade for 0.25 seconds. Admit new actors/props gradually. Door animation
  remains at display refresh, independently of the capped 24 Hz outside view.
- Vehicle art is streamed through the existing worker decoder/staged uploader,
  reduced to small 512-square directional pages and limited to an 8 MiB resident
  cache. Global loader staging, character textures, plant pages, depth buffers
  and CPU world metadata add to this figure; it is not a total-memory claim.

Original feasibility review (historical)

Conclusion
Feasible with the existing isometric warehouse and terrain systems. Build a
restricted exterior view and a procedural sectional shutter. The performance
budget and perspective match still need a native prototype and phone profiling.
This review does not change door behavior, transport scheduling or networking.

Current implementation
- src/bay_door.lua already owns continuous progress from zero to one, open/close
  states, completion events and replicated snapshots. Keep this authority.
- src/outdoor_weather.lua draws a static loadingBayExterior image inside the
  main doorway, with clock-based daylight, night tint and rain.
- src/shop_room_renderer.lua:drawDock lifts a textured polygon sampled from the
  warehouse background. This moves painted shutter content rather than separate
  mechanical sections. The legacy path uses a five-frame sprite strip.
- src/room_loading_bay_renderer.lua provides a second, mirrored room-dock view
  using the same static exterior picture and a separate truck transform.
- src/world_renderer.lua:drawTruck projects the warehouse truck through the
  aperture. Terrain traffic is a different representation of related job state.
- The terrain warehouse has a loading door on its local east wall in
  src/commute/structure_geometry.lua. Sites expose toWorld/toLocal transforms;
  src/commute/sites.lua registers the dock parking and reversing approach.
- src/commute/delivery_traffic.lua:actors exposes outdoor vehicles and NPCs.
  Commute participants expose pedestrians, bikes and player-controlled trucks.
  Player motion snapshots are broadcast to joined clients across scenes.

Recommended rendering
1. Register each dock's world origin, outward direction, width and height,
   alongside its existing painted aperture. Use the actual loading door, not
   the pedestrian entrance or the truck's center, as the camera anchor.
2. Calibrate a fixed exterior camera to the painted threshold and door frame.
   Render a narrow view approximately 9-15 metres (30-50 feet) outward. Cull by
   the visible region and object bounds so partly visible trucks are retained.
   Isometric artwork has no exact physical camera, so check the alignment with
   visible ground markings and a truck crossing the threshold.
3. Cache the local terrain/road/prop geometry and reuse existing texture assets.
   Use the generated town's real surfaces, positions, season and weather. Draw
   only nearby participants, parked vehicles and traffic that can be seen through
   this particular door. Preserve depth occlusion between props and actors.
4. Render into a small reusable canvas, initially 256 x 256, clipped to the
   aperture. The current main opening spans roughly 93 x 126 base-canvas pixels;
   choose resolution from its actual projected size when zoomed. Reuse buffers.
5. Build the shutter from narrow textured sections, with stationary side rails,
   header and sill. Raise sections using door.progress, with restrained easing
   and a visible bottom seal/shadow. Keep the shutter above all exterior actors
   and mask the outside view to the exposed gap throughout opening and closing.
   Animate the door every display frame, independently of exterior refreshes.

Performance approach
- Skip exterior render work when the door is fully closed or its scene is not
  visible. Prepare the small view incrementally as opening begins; retain the
  existing exterior as a temporary loading fallback to avoid a first-open stall.
- Cache geometry rather than rebuilding terrain or scenery every refresh.
  Begin with a capped 20-30 Hz small-view refresh and profile it. Interpolate
  authoritative actor poses and keep warehouse rendering independent.
- Restrict geometry and actor queries before drawing. A scissor or stencil over
  the full outdoor renderer would reduce pixels without bounding its CPU work.
- Do not instantiate src/commute/renderer.lua unchanged for this purpose. Its
  constructor prepares town-wide roads, terrain tiles, structures, vegetation,
  grass and vehicle renderers. Extract/reuse the necessary drawing primitives
  in a restricted view and share already available world data and asset caches.
- A 256 x 256 RGBA8 color buffer contains 0.25 MiB of pixels. Depth/stencil,
  buffering and resident sprite textures add to that figure. Measure total
  residency and frame time on the phone; this is not a measured FPS guarantee.

State and integration details
- Treat this as a viewer of existing state. Do not advance traffic, door logic,
  delivery timers or character movement a second time while drawing the portal.
- Match the warehouse truck and terrain truck by stable shipment/job identity.
  Draw one representation. Preserve the cargo-door and loading view at the dock,
  and align its transition to the terrain truck's backing/parked pose.
- Use existing multiplayer actor state and interpolation. Verify that nearby AI
  traffic continues to receive timely pose updates for an indoor observer. If
  additional updates are necessary, send nearby poses, not images or map data.
- Purchased room docks need their own physical exterior anchors. Their current
  painted apertures and mirrored visual transforms do not define distinct
  outdoor viewing positions. Establish those before claiming accurate views.

Suggested first prototype and acceptance
Prototype the main warehouse door, a small terrain patch and one moving truck
first. Check the door frame, ground plane, truck scale and rear cargo alignment
before extending the view to other players and purchased room docks.
Validate partial-open occlusion; approach/backing/loading/departure continuity;
multiple nearby trucks without duplicates; geared and ordinary pedestrians;
host-inside/guest-outside and the reverse; reconnect; night and rain; seeded
town orientation; repeated opening; and closed-door render inactivity.
Profile first-open stalls, steady frame time and retained texture memory on the
phone with the usual warehouse machinery and multiplayer traffic active.
