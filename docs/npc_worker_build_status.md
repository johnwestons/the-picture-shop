# NPC workers: current implementation

October 6, 2026. Radio Cat, Tinker Fox and Ferret Engineer can be hired to run the cutter, Heidelberg press and skid wrapper through ordered job schedules. The shared desktop/Android game includes shift continuation, negotiated overnight shifts and pay cycles, paid machine training, per-shop clock speed, and a whole-shop staffing budget. Autonomous pallet transport and forklift work remain in the [worker plan](npc_worker_hiring_plan.md).

## Using the feature

1. Open the office computer and use the arrow beside its address bar to choose **HIRING**.
2. Keep recruitment open. Applicants rotate through Radio Cat, Tinker Fox and Ferret Engineer and visit reception on weekdays; request the emailed resume at reception or in **Applicants**. Later applicants are generated at three-to-five game-day intervals, with a single visible reception visitor and at most three open applications.
3. Open the resume attachment from **EMAIL**, or select the applicant in **HIRING**. Offer $10–$100/hour, selected weekdays, a four-to-twelve-hour shift, and a pay cycle of one to four weeks. Start/end hours cover the full day; an end before the start means an overnight shift. Weekdays refer to the day the shift starts. Wait for the reply; the applicant accepts or counters according to their stored wage requirements. A changed offer consumes a negotiation round; repeating identical terms does not. Accepted offers expire after three game days.
4. Sign the latest accepted offer. This creates a contract and staff record and starts on the next upcoming agreed shift. Up to three employees can be hired at once.
5. Choose **SCHEDULE** from the address-bar dropdown and select the employee. **ADD JOB** appends an accepted job whose delivery has arrived and a specific installed cutter. Queue up to 16 jobs, reorder or remove pending work, and use pause/resume as needed. The worker cuts each pallet, runs the Heidelberg when the job requires printing, then wraps the finished pallet before completing the job. Press work requires skill 60; wrapping requires skill 50. Use **HIRING > Staff > TEACH MACHINE SKILLS** to train at an installed machine during paid shift hours. A blocked job stays queued while the worker tries other ready jobs; a running machine cycle finishes safely first. The last 32 completed, removed or unavailable entries appear in history. **HIRING > Staff** still supports one manually assigned pallet; pause the queue first. Use the pallet jack to stage pallets at the machines and clear output. Hard and medium cutter work require the appropriate skill. More skilled, focused workers take less time between operating steps.
6. In **Payroll**, review or pay due wages and compare tracked job labor with idle/break/shop labor. Wages accrue without immediately spending cash and come due Monday 09:00 after the agreed one-to-four-week cycle. The cycle includes the partial first week and is anchored to Monday of the first working week. Waiting at the shop, walking and rest breaks are paid. Meal breaks are unpaid. Overtime still starts after 40 paid hours in each individual week at 1.5×, regardless of pay frequency. Cash shortages make partial payments and retain the exact remaining debt. Dismissal does not erase earned wages. Overdue wages stop new work and seven game days of overdue debt can cause resignation. **Bills** settles the same wage ledger, without a second payroll charge.
7. Choose **Payroll > SHOP BUDGET** to plan completed lifts per week. It budgets the entire agreed shift, including idle time, paid rests and overtime, plus operating bills and machine loans. The estimated capacity respects the number of installed cutters and overlapping employee shifts. Pay-cycle reserves and cash after already-earned wages/bills make longer pay cycles visible as an obligation, rather than extra profit. New employee quotes include a per-lift cost floor for negotiated payroll and machine use; prices already agreed with customers stay fixed. Missing stock, player staging, blocked output and actual customer demand can reduce realized throughput.

Shift time follows the active shop calendar, including while reading the computer. Closing the game does not accrue offline work or payroll. Workers finish an already-running machine cycle safely before leaving or pausing, and that finishing time is paid. An unfinished assignment and its production progress remain saved; the worker resumes on the next agreed shift, including across weekends and save/reload. They receive no wages while off shift. Pause their schedule or assignment before taking over a reserved machine, moving it or selling it. A loaded pallet stays with its job until it reaches the next safe stage. A blocked queued job does not strand the worker when another ready job is available; an active cutter cycle completes safely before the worker changes tasks. A canceled job is recorded as unavailable before advancing.

Customer estimates disclose a staffed-workflow cost based on qualified employees' negotiated pay and operating delays. The budget includes contract-paid rest and weekly overtime, machine overhead and the existing target margin; new quotes also respect the shop budget's per-lift floor. Existing press labor allowances are retained, and only new offers are recalculated. Submitted quotes, accepted jobs and promised promotions retain their agreed prices. Actual paid working intervals are allocated to their job; idle/waiting/break time is allocated to shop labor. These figures record expense and never debit cash separately from payroll.

Paid 15-minute rests occur after two, six and ten hours when the shift lasts beyond that point. Shifts of six or more hours include a 30-minute unpaid meal. Extra rest is triggered by high fatigue or low focus. A completed breakroom with a free seat gives full recovery after the worker actually walks to it; standing rest gives half recovery. Reserved seats cannot also be occupied by a player. Applicant and worker movement follows the walk mask, equipment and other actors.

**Shop Setup Options** appears before creating a save. Players choose any whole-minute day length from five minutes to one hour; new shops default to 20 minutes. Existing shops retain their former five-minute pace. The HUD and computer show live hours/minutes. **SHOP CLOCK** on the computer and the clickable office wall clock show an analog clock and date. The computer calendar includes upcoming shifts and paydays.

The cutter console highlights the actual next margin on the rotating stock and indicates whether rotation is correct. Completed cutter pallets occupy the six numbered slots in the painted front staging rectangle, in row order. Other cutters reserve slots independently. If the area fills during unloading, the stock remains on its cutter and the worker retries when a slot becomes free.

## Saves and multiplayer

Save schema **21** / employment v6 includes per-shop day length, worker profiles, overnight/12-hour contracts, one-to-four-week pay cycles, ordered schedules and training progress. Older saves receive safe defaults while retaining money, stock, contracts, assignments, earned wages and production stock. Local loads stop active worker poses and release reservations before the next shift update. No real-world elapsed time is applied.

LAN protocol **26** uses typed, owner-only hiring/payroll/schedule actions and host-owned simulation. Guests can inspect hiring, staff, schedules, labor costs, the shop budget and clocks. The wall clock is read-only and does not save or claim an exclusive control lease. Bills containing employee wages also require the owner, preventing the ordinary bills action from bypassing payroll permissions. Stable task IDs and revision checks reject duplicate or stale queue edits; the same job cannot be assigned to two employees. Compact realtime NPC poses carry movement direction, gait phase and machine/break action alongside environment snapshots; character profiles, contracts, queues, clock pace and money stay in reliable shop state. NPC identifiers are separate from human player IDs. All participants need matching builds.
