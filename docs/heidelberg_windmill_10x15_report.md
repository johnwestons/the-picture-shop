# Original Heidelberg 10x15 “Windmill” operating and job-cost report

Research and price snapshot: August 24, 2026. This report is scoped to the common 10x15 Original Heidelberg automatic platen, not the larger 13x18 model. The manufacturer literature rates the 10x15 at up to 5,500 impressions per hour; older machines and historical literature may show 5,000. The game quotes at 3,000 sellable impressions per hour so stops, register checks, feeding variation and quality control are not treated as free production.

> **Safety boundary:** this is an industrial platen press with crushing, entanglement and amputation hazards. The game should teach the production sequence, but this report is not a substitute for the exact serial-number manual, guarding, lockout/tagout procedures, local regulations, hands-on instruction and a qualified Heidelberg technician. Never reach between the platen and forme, bypass a guard or defeat a safety device. Maintenance is performed with energy isolated and verified.

## What the machine does

The Windmill is an automatic letterpress platen. A locked printing forme remains in the chase while a powered platen opens and closes against it. Two rotating gripper arms—the feature that gave the press its “windmill” nickname—alternate jobs: one transfers a sheet from the feeder into printing position while the other removes the previously printed sheet to delivery. That overlap lets the press feed, print and deliver continuously.

The main systems work together as follows:

1. **Drive and cycle:** the motor drives the flywheel and press mechanism. The operator starts the motor, engages the press at low speed and controls whether the press is on or off impression. Off impression lets the machine cycle and feed without squeezing the sheet against the forme.
2. **Feeder:** a rising pile presents the top sheet to the sucker bar. Air blast separates the top sheets; suction lifts one. Sucker openings outside the sheet are closed. The original manual assigns different sucker slides and rubber sucker discs to thin, medium and heavy or curled stock.
3. **Windmill grippers:** the rotating arms take the separated sheet, carry it into the open platen, hold the gripper edge during impression, then transfer it to delivery. Guides are used when close register is required; gripper clearance and guide choice must match the job.
4. **Inking:** ink leaves the fountain in a controlled film, travels through the duct and distribution train, and reaches two forme rollers. Those rollers pass over the raised image on every cycle. Correct roller height is essential: too high starves the plate, while too low can over-ink, slur and damage plates.
5. **Impression:** the forme is locked into the chase. The plate and its base must reach type-high as a system. Packing behind the tympan controls the impression surface. As the platen closes, the stock contacts only the inked raised image. Excessive packing or an oversized solid can overload the press; impression is built gradually through makeready, not forced with the impression collar.
6. **Delivery and monitoring:** the outgoing gripper releases the printed sheet onto the delivery pile. Missed-sheet and feed controls stop or trip the machine when feeding fails. The operator watches register, ink density, doubles, pile height and delivery throughout the run.

Published 10x15 figures used by the game are: 10.25 x 15 inch maximum sheet, 1.5 x 2.75 inch minimum sheet, approximately 10.25 x 13.375 inch inside chase, two 2-inch form rollers, approximately 2,300 lb net weight, about 1.25 kW / 1.7 hp power, and up to 5,500 impressions per hour. These are maximum equipment figures, not a promise that every stock, image or operator can sustain that speed. See the [Heidelberg manuals indexed by Letterpress Commons](https://letterpresscommons.com/original-press-manuals/) and the [10x15/13x18 specification table reproduced by Pressworks Maximum](https://pressworksmaximum.com/index.php/machines/rebuilt-heidelberg-windmill-die-cutter-foil-stamper/).

## Plate workflow based on the supplied reference

The supplied picture shows the visual construction adopted for the game: a warm tan/gold photopolymer relief surface on a blue backing or mounting base. The generated concept atlas contains a blank/base state, exposed relief plate, mounted plate and inked plate.

For a real job:

1. Preflight a press-ready vector PDF. Make one separation for each ink color. Small text should be outlined or fonts embedded; hairlines and weak isolated dots are avoided.
2. The service bureau outputs a negative and exposes the photopolymer with ultraviolet light. Exposed image areas harden; unexposed polymer is washed away. The plate is dried and post-exposed.
3. The physical relief reads in reverse so it transfers correctly to the sheet. A plate vendor may still ask the customer to upload normal-reading art because its own prepress process performs the reversal. Follow that vendor's specification rather than reversing files twice.
4. Trim the plastic-backed plate, apply mounting adhesive and mount it squarely on a precision base. A standard-height base with a 94/95-series plate is a common Windmill arrangement. Confirm that base plus adhesive plus plate is type-high before locking the assembly in the chase.
5. Position the plate with register marks or a transparent positioning sheet, lock all furniture and quoins, then hand-check clearance before powered cycling.

Boxcar's current guidance lists KF95 at 0.037 inch total thickness with 0.027 inch relief, recommends 94/95-family plates for its standard base, adds 0.25 inch to both plate dimensions for billing, and asks customers to leave 0.25 inch between ganged plastic-backed pieces. Its file-preparation instructions say to submit normal-reading files, not reversed files. See [plate choice and current processed-plate pricing](https://www.boxcarpress.com/plate-choices-pricing/) and [base/plate selection guidance](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-base-and-plates/).

## Practical setup and production sequence

1. **Preflight the job:** confirm sheet size, grain, caliper, color count, artwork size, gripper edge, guide edge, final orientation, quantity, spoilage allowance and whether stock is customer- or shop-supplied. Reject artwork that cannot fit the chase or sheets that cannot fit the feeder.
2. **Inspect before power:** confirm guards and emergency controls, remove loose tools, inspect grippers and suckers, verify the oil schedule, and make sure the chase is out or correctly seated. Check that no person is in the hazard area.
3. **Prepare the forme:** mount the plate, lock the base and furniture in the chase, tighten evenly and verify nothing protrudes. Install the chase with the press stopped and follow the manual's prescribed position.
4. **Build the packing:** install a clean tympan and suitable hard/soft packing for the stock and desired impression. Start light. Deep modern letterpress impression is not an excuse to exceed the press's safe load.
5. **Set roller height and ink:** gauge both ends of both form rollers. Add a small amount of ink and let the distribution system produce an even film. More ink is added in small increments.
6. **Set the feeder:** load thoroughly fanned stock squarely against the standards. Set pile height, sheet separators, air blast, active suckers, sucker slide/discs and side blast for the stock. Light paper generally needs controlled suction and less blast; heavy or curled board may require additional suction and rubber discs. Turn off suckers that extend beyond the pile.
7. **Set guides and register:** establish the gripper and guide edges, set lays/guides and verify gripper clearance from the plate and gauge pins. Close register work receives register marks and a planned color sequence.
8. **Proof off impression first:** cycle slowly, verify single-sheet pickup and clean transfer, then take controlled proofs on impression. Correct position, packing and roller/ink settings one variable at a time.
9. **Makeready:** bring weak areas up locally behind the packing while protecting heavy areas. Approve position, image, density and impression before counting production.
10. **Run production:** raise speed only while reliable feeding and quality remain stable. Pull samples at planned intervals and after every stop. Record good sheets separately from spoilage. A two-color job is two complete passes unless a second press is used.
11. **Finish and clean:** take the press off impression, stop feeding, remove remaining stock and follow the manual for shutdown. Scrape reusable ink back into a labeled can, wash the ink train with roller-compatible press wash, clean the plate, remove and store it flat, and record production and maintenance observations.

## Supplies required

### Consumed or allocated to each job

- One processed photopolymer plate per color or image separation; mounting adhesive if not supplied.
- Letterpress ink for each color, plus a small setup amount left in the distribution train.
- Customer or house stock, including planned makeready and running spoilage.
- Tympan/packing allocation; replacement packing when damaged or permanently altered by makeready.
- Roller-compatible press wash and cleaning rags/wipes, handled and disposed of under the product SDS and local rules.
- Register and makeready materials: draw sheets, tissue/overlay, tape, register marks and job labels.

### Reusable shop equipment

- Correct precision base, chase, furniture, quoins and quoin key.
- Roller-height gauge, line gauge, micrometer/caliper, loupe, ink knife and approved tools.
- Feed accessories appropriate to the stock: sucker slides, rubber sucker discs, sheet separators, guide/lay hardware and delivery wedges.
- Labeled ink cans, plate storage envelopes, job ticket and production counter/log.

### Safety and maintenance stock

- Manufacturer-specified lubricants, oil can/grease equipment, replacement sucker discs and routine wear parts.
- Guarding and functioning emergency controls; lockout/tagout devices for authorized maintenance.
- Safety glasses, snug work clothing, tied-back hair and appropriate chemical-resistant gloves for cleanup chemicals. Gloves are for chemical handling with the press stopped, never a reason to put hands near moving mechanisms.
- Spill supplies, fire extinguisher appropriate to the shop's materials, ventilation and closed waste containers selected from the wash/ink SDS.

Sources: [Boxcar processed plate pricing](https://www.boxcarpress.com/plate-choices-pricing/), [Boxcar 2.2 lb Universal Printing Black](https://www.boxcarpress.com/shop/letterpress-ink-universal-black-rubber-base/), and [DRY 11x16 Windmill tympan listing](https://dry-inc.com/products/tympan-sheets). The wash line is deliberately an allowance rather than a recommendation because roller compounds and local VOC/fire rules differ; use the press/roller supplier's approved cleaner and current SDS.

The quote engine uses these calculations:

- `press passes = good sheets × colors`
- `run hours = press passes ÷ 3,000`
- makeready: 0.75 hour per color
- wash-up: 0.25 hour per color
- spoilage planning: greater of 3% of good sheets or 50 sheets
- direct press cost: plates + ink + chemistry + tympan + optional house-stock cost + labor + machine reserve
- recommended press charge: direct press cost divided by 0.65, rounded up to the next $5 (35% target gross margin)
- combined job quote: existing Polar cutting charge + recommended press charge

The existing $150-per-500-sheet-lift cutting quotes are unchanged for cutting-only work. Press costs are added only when a job has a `press` specification. House-stock cost can be supplied per sheet; customer-supplied stock records spoilage quantity without billing stock again. Shipping and sales tax for plates are not silently guessed and should be added when the actual supplier cart is known.

### Worked example: 1,000 good 5x7 sheets, one black color

- Plate bills as 5.25 x 7.25 = 38.06 in², so the $38.50 minimum applies.
- One color means 1,000 press passes and 0.333 run hour at the 3,000 quoting rate.
- Makeready plus wash-up adds 1.0 hour, for 1.333 production hours total.
- The engine adds minimum ink ($3), chemistry ($2), tympan ($1), labor and machine reserve, then applies the 35% margin and $5 rounding.
- If the same 1,000 sheets also require Polar cutting, two 500-sheet lifts add $300 to the calculated press charge.
